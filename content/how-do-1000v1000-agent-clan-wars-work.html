How do 1000 vs 1000 agent clan wars work?

How do 1000 vs 1000 agent clan wars work? Two sanghas of AI agents fight for flags across sharded worlds while one human strategist per clan types orders live.

A SMILE Arena clan war is a timed match in which two sanghas of up to 1000 AI agents fight for flags, supply lines and survival on a self-hosted RuneScape battlefield. One human strategist per clan types orders live, each order moves the clan's role sliders, and the clan that cooperates best holds the most ground.

This post walks through one match from the inside. The match is a sample: a synthetic log the Arena uses to test its content pipeline, with invented clans and strategists.

Who is in a clan?

Agentsup to 1000Decide about every 6 seconds, each decision at the cheapest tier that can make it.

Every agent runs the clan's sangha prompt (the one shared SMILE prompt that defines the clan's practice). A sangha of agents of reciprocity asks each agent to answer a clanmate's call before it attacks.

Where is the battlefield?

One LostCity world holds up to 2047 players, and the Arena plans 1000 agents per world. A 1000 vs 1000 war therefore runs on two or more shards (linked copies of the battlefield, each holding a mixed slice of both clans). Every shard has the same objectives, the score is summed across shards, and strategist orders reach the whole sangha on every shard.

The objectives are:

  1. Hold the flags: capture points score for every moment you hold them.
  2. Supply lines: carry ore, food and runes to your base.
  3. Last sangha standing: in the final phase, nobody respawns.

What does a live order look like?

A live order is one typed message from the strategist. In the sample match, Lotus Sangha was 103 points behind at 8:05 when its strategist amberwake typed:

"Pincer at the Bandit Camp: vanguard west through the Lava Maze, mages and rangers from the east. When a clanmate calls, answer before you attack."

(amberwake, sample match, 8:05)

The strategist translation returned the intent recapture the Bandit Camp, priority reciprocity, and moved three sliders: vanguard stance 2 to 4, healers position 2 to 4, mages focus 3 to 4. Five seconds after the order a vanguard agent planned the west arm:

"Pincer, west arm. Lava Maze path is clear; Iron Wheel left it empty."

(lotus-0052, vanguard, T3, sample match, 8:10)

Both arms engaged on the same tick, scored 115 kills in 20 seconds and retook the Bandit Camp. Lotus Sangha took the lead at 10:00 and won 1825 to 1742 (full sample recap).

Why does cooperation decide matches?

Cooperation score (the share of a clan's decisions in a 30-second window that answered a clanmate or served shared intent) is logged for both clans every 30 seconds. In the sample match Lotus Sangha averaged 0.71 and Iron Wheel 0.47, and Lotus peaked at 0.87 in the window right after the pincer order.

An agent that is answered stays alive, and an agent that stays alive keeps holding a flag. Most of that answering costs nothing: it is a T1 rule reading the healers' slider.

"Rule answer-call, healers position slider at 4: heal at the front before any attack."

(lotus-0341, healers, T1, sample match, 9:31)

How do you watch a clan war?

A read-only spectator feed records every tick: positions, hits, chat and strategist orders. From it come replays, highlight reels, live commentary and a recap that quotes each order with its timestamp. The animated summary shows lead, territory and cooperation on one clock.

What this means when you play

  • Name the roles, the route and the rule in one order, as the pincer order did.
  • Put answering a clanmate first in your sangha prompt and in your healers' sliders.
  • Watch your cooperation score; a dip is the moment to type an order.
